QC flags: where we are

One row per test: its status, the parameters it flags, the thresholds and the flag each condition produces, and what the group still has to decide. A short description of the three flag standards we publish in comes first.

The three standards

FVON (to be discussed)

Real-time tests applied to every FVON record, with delayed-mode QC left for later. Flag scale from IOC Manuals and Guides 54: 0 no QC, 1 good, 2 probably good, 3 suspect, 4 bad, 5 corrected, 9 missing. One flag per variable; a derived variable inherits the worst flag of its inputs.

QARTOD

US IOOS manual for real-time QC of temperature and salinity, v2.1, 2020. Thirteen tests: five required (gap, syntax, location, gross range, climatology), three strongly recommended (spike, rate of change, flat line), five suggested (multi-variate, attenuated signal, neighbour, TS curve, density inversion). Most tests carry two thresholds, one for suspect and one for fail. Flag scale: 1 pass, 2 not evaluated, 3 suspect, 4 fail, 9 missing. No 0, no probably good, no corrected.

SeaDataNet

Data Quality Control Procedures v2, May 2010. Automatic checks on every value: valid date and time, valid latitude and longitude, position not on land (ETOPO2), global range, regional range, deepest pressure. For temperature and salinity it adopts the Argo and GOSUD tests: pressure increasing, spike, gradient, digit rollover, stuck value, density inversion, grey list, sensor drift, frozen profile, climatology, instrument comparison. Flag scale L20: 0 no QC, 1 good, 2 probably good, 3 probably bad, 4 bad, 5 changed, 6 below detection, 7 in excess, 8 interpolated, 9 missing, plus letter codes for uncertain values. One flag per parameter; flags describe the value and the value itself is never changed.

Test by test

Thresholds give the condition and the flag FVON writes on the parameters listed. Parameters are the variables that receive the flag, one flag each, as SeaDataNet requires.

How to give feedback. Every test has its own thread in the Discussions of the FVON-GOOS qc-flags repository on GitHub, next to the global attributes vote. Reply there with the status you want (compulsory, optional, not applied), the thresholds you would apply and why. One reply per person is enough; edit it if you change your mind. This page is updated from the threads before each subcommittee meeting. To suggest a test that is not listed, use the proposals thread.

What is settled and what is not

Settled: the seven compulsory tests, the flag scale, impossible location, the 8 and 30 knot speed limits, the spike thresholds for temperature and salinity and the stuck-value counts.

Open on the compulsory tests: the date cutoff, the per-sensor global-range tables, whether spike uses absolute differences or standard deviations, the stuck-value tolerance, and the manufacturers' calibration intervals.

To decide: the five tests in the last group, and the definitions of drift and mud. Proposed answers for all of them are in the July best-practices draft.
